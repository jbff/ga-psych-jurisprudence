- **D)** A maximum of 15 CE credits per article, up to a total of 30 CE credits per biennium

### Q2. Under Board Rule 510-9-.01, who is eligible to apply for a temporary psychology license in Georgia?

- **A)** Recent doctoral graduates who are currently completing their predoctoral internships
- **B)** Applicants who have submitted a completed application for licensure by endorsement
- **C)** Licensure candidates completing their postdoctoral supervised work experience
- **D)** Out-of-state psychologists seeking uncompensated volunteer practice in disaster areas

### Q3. Under Board Rule 510-8-.02, what is the maximum number of continuing education credits per biennium that may be earned through Area II group consultation?

- **A)** 20 CE credits per biennium
- **B)** 30 CE credits per biennium
- **C)** 15 CE credits per biennium
- **D)** 10 CE credits per biennium

### Q4. If licensed in the second year of the biennium, how many CE credits are required for first renewal?

- **A)** 6 credits in professional ethics only
- **B)** 20 credits, including 3 ethics credits
- **C)** No continuing education credits are required for the first renewal cycle
- **D)** 10 credits, with no ethics requirement

### Q5. What is 'child abuse' under Georgia law?

- **A)** Normal childhood conflicts between siblings that cause emotional distress
- **B)** Physical injury or death inflicted by a parent/custodian/other caretaker
- **C)** Truancy or willful refusal to attend public school without parental consent
- **D)** Reasonable corporal punishment administered by a parent or legal guardian

### Q6. Under APA Ethics Code Standard 3.09 (Rule 510-4-.02), what is a psychologist's ethical responsibility when cooperation with other professionals is indicated?

- **A)** Cooperate with other professionals in order to serve their clients/patients effectively and appropriately
- **B)** Demand sole decision-making authority and refuse to share any clinical treatment summaries
- **C)** Condition cooperation on the other professional adopting the psychologist's theoretical orientation
- **D)** Require the client to terminate all contact with other medical providers before initiating therapy

### Q7. Under O.C.G.A. § 30-5-4(b)(2), by what means may a mandated report of suspected disabled adult or elder abuse be submitted?

- **A)** Exclusively by formal verified petition filed in the probate court
- **B)** By oral or written communication
- **C)** Exclusively by in-person personal appearance at the county adult protection office
- **D)** Exclusively by certified mail with return receipt requested

### Q8. Under O.C.G.A. § 30-5-4(d), does statutory or professional privilege prevent mandatory reporting of elder or disabled adult abuse?

- **A)** No; however, reporting is excused if the psychologist determines that reporting would harm therapeutic rapport
- **B)** Yes; communications between a psychologist and an adult client are completely confidential and immune from reporting
- **C)** No; abuse, neglect, or exploitation must be reported notwithstanding any privileged communication (except clergy confession)
- **D)** Yes; reporting is prohibited unless the disabled adult or elder person provides signed informed consent to report

### Q9. What privilege applies to psychologist-client communications in Georgia?

- **A)** Work-product doctrine applicable to civil litigation
- **B)** Qualified peer review immunity under hospital regulations
- **C)** Same as attorney-client privilege
- **D)** Physician-patient privilege under common law

### Q10. What other professionals' communications are privileged under OCGA 24-9-21?

- **A)** Physical therapists, occupational therapists, speech-language pathologists, and medical assistants
- **B)** Attorneys, clergy, and certified public accountants only
- **C)** Psychiatrist-patient; licensed clinical social worker, clinical nurse specialist psych/mental health, marriage and family therapist, professional counselor during psychotherapeutic relationship
- **D)** Registered nurses, hospital administrators, medical records clerks, and case managers

### Q11. Under Board Rule 510-2-.05, may a psychologist serve as supervisor if they are employed by an entity in which the supervisee has a financial or ownership interest?

- **A)** Yes; provided the supervisee's ownership share in the clinical entity is less than 50%
- **B)** Yes; provided secondary supervision is completed by an independent licensed physician
- **C)** No; a supervisor cannot be employed by an entity in which the supervisee holds a proprietary or ownership interest
- **D)** Yes; if the arrangement is formally registered with the Secretary of State Corporations Division

### Q12. Under Board Rule 510-5-.02, which tax reporting form generally reflects an employer-employee relationship for clinical personnel?

- **A)** IRS Form W-2 (Wage and Tax Statement)
- **B)** IRS Form 1040 Schedule C (Profit or Loss from Business)
- **C)** IRS Form 990 (Return of Organization Exempt From Income Tax)
- **D)** IRS Form 1099-NEC (Nonemployee Compensation)

### Q13. Under APA Ethics Code Standard 3.11(b) (Rule 510-4-.02), what must psychologists do if organizational roles or law preclude providing feedback or disclosure to individuals?

- **A)** Inform the affected individuals and groups of that restriction at the outset of the service
- **B)** File a formal ethics complaint against the organization with the Georgia Department of Labor
- **C)** Withhold all evaluation findings from both the individuals and the organizational leadership
- **D)** Disclose the confidential findings secretly to the individuals despite organizational agreements

### Q14. May psychologists accept as clients persons with whom they had prior sexual intimacies?

- **A)** Yes, in emergency crisis situations where other providers are unavailable
- **B)** Yes, if at least two years have elapsed since the prior sexual relationship
- **C)** Yes, provided another psychologist conducts concurrent supervision
- **D)** No; psychologists do not accept as therapy clients persons with whom they have engaged in sexual intimacies

### Q15. Under Board Rule 510-2-.05, which licensure fellows are exempt from the standard 1-hour-per-30-hours SWE supervision ratio?

- **A)** Industrial-Organizational (I/O) psychology fellows
- **B)** School psychology postdoctoral fellows
- **C)** Forensic psychology postdoctoral fellows
- **D)** Clinical neuropsychology postdoctoral fellows

### Q16. May the division director disclose information regarding pending investigations to other licensing authorities?

- **A)** Only upon issuance of a formal subpoena by a federal regulatory or law enforcement agency
- **B)** Only if the investigated licensee executes a notarized consent form authorizing the disclosure
- **C)** Yes; authorized to provide investigative and disciplinary information to any lawful licensing authority upon inquiry
- **D)** No; investigative information is strictly confidential and cannot be disclosed before a formal consent order is executed

### Q17. Under O.C.G.A. § 43-39-8, what is required before any person may engage in the practice of psychology in Georgia?

- **A)** Submit a formal application to the Board, meet statutory qualifications, and obtain a valid license
- **B)** Register a clinical business trade name with the county superior court clerk only
- **C)** Obtain a diploma from an accredited doctoral psychology program without applying to the Board
- **D)** File a surety bond of $100,000 with the Office of the Georgia Secretary of State

### Q18. What must records include?

- **B)** Photographs of the client and personal contact details of all extended family members
- **C)** Client personal tax returns and detailed credit card account histories
- **D)** Identifying/contact data, fees/billing, informed consent, diagnosis/basis, plan, dates/services, interventions, assessment

- **B)** Charging different sliding-scale hourly professional fees based on verified household income levels
- **C)** Referring clients with complex medical comorbidities to board-certified medical specialists
- **D)** Unfair discrimination and harassment or demeaning conduct based on protected personal characteristics

- **B)** Generalized medical codes that conceal mental health diagnoses
- **C)** Nature of service, fees, provider identity, findings, diagnosis
- **D)** Any billing code that maximizes insurance reimbursement for the client
